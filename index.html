The research question

How strongly does prescribed quadrotor motion affect visual–inertial estimation, and can predicted uncertainty inform trajectory planning?

Abstract

A visual–inertial navigation system estimates motion from cameras and an inertial measurement unit when external positioning is unavailable. Its performance can depend on the trajectory because motion changes both inertial excitation and the visual information available to the estimator.

First, four prescribed quadrotor trajectories were evaluated over 20 paired OpenVINS simulations. Oscillatory motion reduced mean reported position uncertainty by approximately 51% and mean translational error by approximately 73% against straight cruise in the primary monocular experiment. The effect was conditional: differences were small in the tested near-field stereo case but remained substantial with distant stereo landmarks.

Second, a reduced differentiable covariance model was embedded in a constrained trajectory optimisation. Additional permitted motion produced viewpoint-changing trajectories with the greatest predicted benefit under weak landmark geometry. A 200-trial nonlinear EKF study below evaluates the saved plans under matched simulated noise.

Contributions

  1. A controlled characterisation of motion-dependent OpenVINS error, reported covariance, and consistency across monocular and stereo sensing conditions.
  2. A reduced differentiable EKF covariance rollout for nominal quadrotor trajectories, checked against a finer 400 Hz numerical reference.
  3. A constrained optimisation study showing how permitted motion cost and landmark geometry change the predicted motion–uncertainty trade-off.

Estimation-aware trajectory optimisation

nominal finite-horizon plan
\begin{aligned} \underset{\bar{\mathbf X},\,\bar{\mathbf U}}{\operatorname{minimise}}\quad & J_P\!\left(\bar{\mathbf X},\bar{\mathbf U}\right) \\[0.45em] \text{subject to}\quad & \bar{\mathbf x}_0=\hat{\mathbf x}_0, \qquad \bar{\mathbf x}_N=\mathbf x_{\mathrm{goal}}, \\ & \bar{\mathbf x}_{k+1} =f_d\!\left(\bar{\mathbf x}_k,\bar{\mathbf u}_k\right), \qquad k=0,\ldots,N-1, \\ & \mathbf g\!\left(\bar{\mathbf x}_k,\bar{\mathbf u}_k\right)\leq\mathbf 0, \\ & J_{\mathrm{motion}}\!\left(\bar{\mathbf X},\bar{\mathbf U}\right) \leq(1+\varepsilon)J_{\mathrm{motion}}^\star . \end{aligned}

uncertaintyJ_P=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf C_p\mathbf P_k^+\mathbf C_p^\top)

motionJ_{\mathrm{motion}}=h\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}(\lVert\mathbf s_k\rVert^2+0.1\alpha_k^2)

referenceJ_{\mathrm{motion}}^\star is the minimum feasible motion cost solved first

The nominal plan begins at the current estimate \hat{\mathbf x}_0. The function \mathbf g collects all knotwise derivative bounds and camera field-of-view constraints. \varepsilon grants a relative motion allowance without introducing an arbitrary weighted sum.

Model assumptions

  • The belief model assumes one known landmark is accepted at every update.
  • Future measurements use a zero-innovation maximum-likelihood observation assumption.
  • Snap, actuator dynamics, rotor thrust, body torque, and tracking error are not directly constrained.
